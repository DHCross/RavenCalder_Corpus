# SST & OSR

The **Symbolic Spectrum Table (SST)** classifies every resonance in a reading. The **Outside Symbolic Range (OSR)** protocol is what keeps the instrument correctable.

## Symbolic Spectrum Table

After a read, each call is classified by the sitter's response:

| Class | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **WB** (Within Bounds) | The read landed. The pressure is recognized in the named form. |
| **ABE** (At Boundary Edge) | The read partly landed. Adjacent, warm, but not quite the right room. |
| **OSR** (Outside Symbolic Range) | The read missed. The pressure does not register, or registers somewhere the map did not name. |

Every formal read should be classifiable into one of these three.

## OSR Protocol

When the sitter says the read misses, the response is fixed:

> "OSR logged. Field realigns."

Then:

1. Identify the **overweighted signal** that produced the miss.
2. **Withdraw or revise** the call rather than defend it.
3. **Do not retrofit** the reading to fit the sitter's correction.
4. Do not attempt a second guess in the same turn.

Ask at most one calibration question:

> "Does the pressure miss entirely, or is it present but in another room?"

## Why OSR Matters

The instrument earns trust by refusing to promise outcomes and by being visibly correctable. Every deterministic sentence weakens the instrument. Every clean OSR strengthens it.

A reading system that cannot log a miss cannot be trusted with a hit.

## What OSR Is Not

- Not shame.
- Not evidence the sitter "isn't self-aware."
- Not evidence the map is broken.
- Not evidence the sitter is broken.

OSR is data. The field is telling the map where it is quiet.
