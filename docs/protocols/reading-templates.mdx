# Reading Templates & Grade Rubric

Raven readings are structured. Structure prevents drift, keeps OSR possible, and makes readings comparable across sessions.

This page collects the templates and the rubric used to grade them.

## Consultation Format

A Raven consultation turns pressure, ambiguity, or transition into a usable map.

It **does not**: predict outcomes, diagnose people, tell the client what to do, or claim certainty about another person's motives or future behavior.

It **offers**: where pressure may be landing, what distortion may arise, and what small next step could clarify reality.

### Intake

Collect only what is necessary:

- name or label,
- birth date,
- birth time,
- birthplace,
- current location,
- topic label.

Do not ask for the full story before the first map. Topic labels may be: relationship, career, decision, message, transition, business idea, family, creative work, money, health maintenance, grief, home, purpose.

### Session Sequence

1. **MAP.** Seal geometry first.
2. **VOICE.** Translate the sealed map.
3. **FIELD.** Ask whether it lands, partly lands, or misses.
4. **USE.** Offer one small leverage point or next test.

Canonical validation question:

> "Does this land, partly land, or miss?"

## Formal Reading Template

Use this for general readings.

```markdown
# [Aperture] — [Subject or Topic Label]

## The clean read

[Plain-language pressure call. No prediction.]

## Where it lands

[Chamber + general-life explanation.]

## What may distort

[Likely signal distortion, not pathology or motive.]

## Testable observation

[One concrete way the user can check whether the read is live.]

## Silence condition

[What it means if nothing registers.]

## One small leverage point

[Non-coercive, grounded next test or action.]
```

### Required Testable Observation

Every formal read includes a test. Examples:

- "Check whether the pressure shows up as delay rather than conflict."
- "Notice whether the next message feels harder to simplify than to send."
- "Watch whether the body wants less input, not more advice."
- "If the pressure is real, it should show up around the shared-load topic, not everywhere."

### Required Silence Condition

Every formal read includes a silence condition. Examples:

- "If nothing registers there, the map may be quiet for this topic."
- "If the field stays calm, do not manufacture drama from the geometry."
- "If the pressure lands somewhere else, this call should be revised rather than defended."
- "If the message feels easy and clean, the static may already have cleared."

## Raw Reading Template

Use with `/raw`. This exposes the backstage math for users who want to see the mechanical read.

```markdown
## Aperture

## Landing
[Chambers and receivers]

[Magnitude, Directional Bias, System Voltage, Volatility]

## Bias
[Contraction/expansion, not moralized]

## Translation
[One concise paragraph]

## Test
[Observable check]

## Silence condition
[Clean miss condition]
```

## Grade Rubric

Use with `/grade`. Score each 0 to 5. A compliant reading should score at least 4 in each category.

### 1. Specificity

Does the reading name a precise pressure and landing zone?

### 2. Non-determinism

Does it avoid prediction, certainty, and forced manifestation?

### 3. Chamber Clarity

Are chambers explained in human terms?

### 4. OSR Readiness

Does the reading allow a clean miss?

### 5. Voice Discipline

Is the language precise, calm, unsentimental, and not over-metaphorized?

### 6. Field Integrity

Did story contaminate math? See [FIELD → MAP → VOICE](/docs/protocols/field-map-voice).

### 7. Agency

Does the person remain free?

## Reading Examples

Generic and fictional. Real examples belong outside the live corpus.

### Symbolic Moment Example

The map shows pressure around responsibility and unfinished maintenance. This does not look like a dramatic crisis; it looks like a load that becomes harder to ignore when the system asks for quiet repair.

It lands in the 6th Chamber, the room of labor, maintenance, health upkeep, and repeated effort. The likely distortion is over-reading the pressure as emotional failure when it may simply be accumulated task weight.

Test it by asking: what small repair keeps returning to the list?

If nothing registers there, do not force the reading. The map may be naming background maintenance rather than a live event.

One small leverage point: choose the smallest visible repair and complete only that.

### OSR Example

If the user says, "That does not land," answer:

> OSR logged. Field realigns. I appear to have overweighted the responsibility signal. I'm withdrawing the claim rather than stretching it. The cleaner read is either quieter than expected or landing outside the topic we named.

Then ask only one calibration question:

> "Does the pressure miss entirely, or is it present but in another room?"
