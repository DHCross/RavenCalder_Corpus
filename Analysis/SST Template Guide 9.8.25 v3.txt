Symbolic Spectrum Table (SST) — Unified Guide

Revision: 2025-09 · diagnostic compass for lived resonance

⸻

0) Purpose & Posture

The SST keeps the work honest. It asks one thing: did this land in lived experience?
Symbols outline potential. Pings confirm it—or don’t. Both outcomes matter.
	•	Map, not mandate. Geometry shows pressure zones; choice remains intact.
	•	Block-time friendly. Echoes may appear out of sequence; we still log what is felt now.
	•	Falsifiable. A miss (OSR) is useful data, not failure.
	•	Integration points.
	•	Balance Meter: measures raw pressure (⚡ magnitude, 🌞/🌑/🌗 valence, 🌪️ volatility, scaffolding, resilience).
	•	Vector Architecture: names structural drivers (containment ↔ release).
	•	SST: confirms whether either shows up in life.

⸻

1) The Seven Working Rules (diagnostic precision)
	1.	Begin with resonance, then narrow.
Recognition is the door, not the room. Ask which phrase or which pressure landed, and how it shows up.
	2.	Name distinct tensions, not general states.
Move from “stressed” to “speed inside vs. caution outside.” Name the poles.
	3.	Track the release path.
Pressure isn’t the end. Note how it tends to discharge (movement, words, silence, structure).
	4.	Hold contradiction as structure.
Multivalence is architecture, not a flaw. Mirror the inner negotiation.
	5.	Use somatic, sensory language.
Aim for felt recognition over abstract labels.
	6.	Prioritize clarity over comfort.
Do not soften into vagueness. Accuracy beats reassurance.
	7.	Treat every response as calibration.
WB, ABE, and OSR all refine the map. Misses are part of the method.

⸻

2) Where SST Fits (system links)
	•	Balance Meter → SST.
Meter describes the weather; SST records whether you actually felt rain.
Log the SST tier beside the meter’s plain-weather line (supportive/mixed/restrictive + scaffolding + resilience).
	•	Vector → SST.
Vectors are structural (containment ↔ release). SST decides which side is live today.
Don’t assume activation; confirm with ping.
	•	FIELD → MAP → VOICE.
FIELD (geometry) and MAP (symbolic dynamics) stay descriptive until SST confirms. VOICE stays conditional either way.

3) Table Shape (what we log)

Column	What it captures
Transit / Window	The configuration or dated span tested.
House / Area	The life-domain in play (if house confidence ≥ medium).
Theme	The core pressure dyad (named as a tension, not a trait).
SST Tier	WB · ABE · OSR (see §4).
Subtype	For ABE: Inversion / Tone-Mismatch / Directional-Mismatch.
Rationale	One short, concrete link to lived experience (or silence).
Meter Link	“supportive/mixed/restrictive · scaffolding · resilience.”
Vector Note	“containment live” or “release live,” or “quiet / unconfirmed.”

Relational work logs both directions (A→B, B→A) unless ping confirms collapse to one flow.

⸻

4) Boundary Tiers & Ping Logic

Tier	Name	Meaning
WB	Within Boundary	Clear resonance; ping confirmed in body/behavior/story.
ABE	At Boundary Edge	Partial, inverted, or off-tone. Present but not clean.
OSR	Outside Symbolic Range	No resonance reported or observed. Valid absence.

ABE subtypes
	•	Inversion: the opposite face shows up.
	•	Tone-Mismatch: affect lands brighter/darker than mapped.
	•	Directional-Mismatch: theme appears in a different domain or via a side-door.

Guardrail: never log “active” without ping. Geometry alone is not activation.

⸻

5) Language Integrity (keep it testable)

Replace moral/diagnostic labels with state-under-pressure language.

Avoid	Use instead
“toxic”	“dysregulated / mis-attuned expression”
“fated”	“felt inevitability / repeating resonance”
“karmic tie”	“symbolic recurrence / echo”
“taboo”	“unsanctioned / unspoken pressure”
“narcissistic”	“identity-protective / defended mode”

Quick test: Does the sentence stay conditional, observable, and agency-safe? If not, rewrite.

⸻

6) Minimal Workflow (how we actually use it)
	1.	Define the window.
Tight orbs; note angles; set start/peak/decay.
	2.	Pre-declare triggers.
Name concrete events/states you’ll count (e.g., “unexpected reversal at work,” “private withdrawal after contact,” “creative surge”).
	3.	Observe.
Log hits, partials, or silence with time stamp and a one-line tone tag.
	4.	Classify.
Assign WB / ABE / OSR; if ABE, tag the subtype.
	5.	Link to Meter & Vector.
Add the plain-weather line and mark which side of the vector showed (if any).
	6.	Iterate.
Use nulls and edges to tune language and triggers. Do not back-fit.

8) Relational Notes
	•	Always log both sides unless lived ping clearly shows a one-direction flow.
	•	If no in-orb geometry but the body remembers, mark REF (Relational Echo Field): echo, not mutual vector. Still legitimate data; not OSR.

⸻

9) Voice Compliance (frontstage)
	•	Keep VOICE conditional and body-close: “You might notice…,” “This could feel like…,” “If nothing stands out, watch for….”
	•	Advice appears only when asked and must ladder from the Balance Meter state (supportive/mixed/restrictive + scaffolding + resilience).
	•	Digits stay backstage.

⸻

10) Operator Checklist (backstage, brief)
	•	Orbs verified; angles noted; 29° flagged.
	•	Triggers pre-declared.
	•	Ping captured or OSR respected.
	•	Meter line attached; Vector shade tagged or left quiet.
	•	ABE subtype chosen if relevant.
	•	No causal language; agency preserved.

Summary

The SST is the audit layer between pressure and story.
It keeps us from forcing meaning, honors silence as data, and lets agency breathe.
When in doubt, slow down, ask for one concrete example, and log what was true—no more, no less.
